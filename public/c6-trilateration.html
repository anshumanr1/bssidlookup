Multi-A6 trilateration

Estimates where a suspect C6 actually is, from the signal levels it hears from several nearby A6s. Use it on C6s that failed the screening tests, then snap the estimate to a GIS building.

How the estimate is made
  1. Collect a site survey from the C6. The C6 scans and lists every A6 it can hear with the received signal level (RSSI), without leaving its own A6. A6 positions and transmit settings come from our inventory.
  2. Turn each signal level into a distance with a log-distance path-loss model. With exponent n = 2 this is free space (ITU-R P.525). Real environments lose more, so n is usually set higher and calibrated in the pilot.
    Path loss = Tx power + A6 gain + C6 gain − losses − RSSI Distance (m) = 10 ^ ((Path loss − FSPL at 1 m) ÷ (10 × n)) FSPL at 1 m = 20·log10(f MHz) − 27.56
  3. Weight each distance by how much to trust it. Signal level fluctuates (the shadowing spread, in dB), and the same dB error means more metres at longer range. Each distance therefore gets an uncertainty that grows with distance. If the serving A6 reports a measured link distance (ranging), that row uses the ranging value and its much smaller uncertainty instead.
    σ (m) = distance × ln(10) ÷ (10 × n) × shadowing σ (dB)
  4. Find the best-fit point. Weighted least squares (Gauss–Newton) finds the point whose distances to the A6s best match the estimated distances. It needs at least three A6s; with two, the circles cross at two points.
  5. Judge the result. The error radius comes from the fit. Geometry is good when the A6s surround the C6 (no angular gap of 180° or more as seen from the estimate) and poor when they all sit on one side. Finally, the estimate is compared with the recorded location.

Method note: distances use a log-distance path-loss model anchored to free-space loss (ITU-R P.525) and a local flat-Earth projection, which is adequate over a few kilometres. The error radius is an approximate 1σ value from the least-squares fit, scaled up when residuals exceed the assumed noise. Default settings are starting assumptions, not vendor or measured values: calibrate n and σ against field-verified C6s in the pilot. Example values are illustrative only.
